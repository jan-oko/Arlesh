- **Edit one habit occurrence on its own.** Double-click a habit occurrence (not the iteration itself) on the Mindmap or in the List View to change just that one: its title, a block reason, and — for a task — its Plan and what it waits on in that iteration. Next time round it reads the habit's template again. Setting a field back to the template's hands it back: retype the usual title, empty the block reason, choose "Follow the Cycle Plan", or tick the usual dependencies. One save is one undo step.
  The Plan can be a day, a part of a day or a range, picked with the same Plan field a Task uses, or left deliberately unplanned this time. The calendar badge shows the plan the occurrence actually has. It is drawn in the accent colour when the plan is that occurrence's own, and struck through when you took the plan away. A plan outside the occurrence's own window is refused, and a future occurrence can be planned ahead.
  A habit's dependencies now hold up its occurrences the way they hold up a started flow's steps. An occurrence reads blocked while something it waits on in that iteration is unfinished, and one iteration can add or drop a dependency without touching the rest.
  Delete on a habit occurrence now deletes it from that iteration only, instead of refusing. The iteration no longer waits for it, and nothing it held up stays blocked. It is hidden everywhere except the All preset, where its archive badge says it was deleted, and its editor restores it. Undo also brings it back. An occurrence that still holds something is not deleted until that is deleted first.
  Editing a habit's scope or repetition now asks before discarding any of these edits.
  Saving a habit's step no longer throws away what you recorded on its occurrences. A title-only save changes nothing about them, and changing a cycle's plan keeps them. If a cycle change would still leave recorded work behind, you get the same "Archive & new" or "Discard & regenerate" question the habit editor asks.
