- **Phone layout.** Below 700 px wide — a narrow window, or a phone — Arlesh lays itself out for a
  small screen: the views move to a bottom bar (Zen, List, Plan, Steps, Map), the preset becomes a
  chip in the top bar that opens a picker, and a ☰ menu holds Filter, the Plan scope, Settings and
  the keyboard sheet. Editors and searches fill the screen, and menus, pickers and prompts rise from
  the bottom as sheets. List rows take two lines, with their path headers pinned while you scroll.
  The tab strip is hidden there; the tab keys still work from a keyboard.
- **Action bar and command palette.** In the phone layout, and on any touch-only screen, a bar above
  the views offers what the selection most often needs — the status cycle, a child, Plan, a
  dependency for a Task; the verdict for a Commitment; New, Undo, Redo, search and the filter with
  nothing selected — and More opens a palette of every command that can act right now, each with its
  key. They run exactly what the keys run, and the keys keep working whenever a keyboard is attached.
