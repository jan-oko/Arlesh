- **Filter the board to one period, and say what you mean by "in" it.** A new scope control sits in the top bar beside the status preset, in every view, reading the period you are looking at or **Any scope**. Pick a scope — or a range — from the usual calendar, and choose two things about it.

  **Match on** decides which window is compared: **Relevance**, the item's own Time Scope or, when it has none, the nearest scoped ancestor's; or **Plan**, where a Task is actually scheduled. A Plan is never inherited, so "what is planned into next Tuesday" answers with Tasks that are planned into it and nothing else.

  **Rule** decides what counts as being in the scope. **Within** keeps only what the period wholly holds — what belongs to exactly this week. **Overlapping** keeps everything that shares any moment with it, so the season-scoped goal that spans the week comes along; that is the question a planning pass asks, and it is what a new selection starts on. An unscoped item is always relevant, so it shows under Overlapping and never under Within.

  The selection sits alongside your other filters and belongs to the tab, so two tabs can hold two different weeks at once and both come back after a restart. It narrows the board on top of the status preset and the Scope pills rather than replacing either, there is one at a time, and the × beside it clears it without opening anything. Agents see the same thing: `arlesh_snapshot` takes the same selection, so asking what is relevant in a given week gets the answer that is on your screen.

  The Plan View's two columns are unchanged. They already ask these questions — the right-hand pane is Plan within the scope, the left is Relevance overlapping it — and they now share one implementation with the selector rather than repeating it.
