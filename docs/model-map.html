Arlesh · conceptual model

How Arlesh fits together

Arlesh stores very little and derives a great deal. This page is the map: what is written down, what is worked out from it on every load, and how that reaches the screen and the agents. Each card says why a concept exists and what would break without it.

It is not the specification. The rules themselves live in SPEC.md and docs/spec/; an agent-friendly copy of this page, with fuller code pointers, is model-map.md.

1 · What's stored

The few things written down

Rows of each kind, the tree they hang in, the templates that generate work, and the edits made to generated work.

Node kinds

Six fixed Aspects at the top, then Projects, Domains and Tags to organise; Goals (desired states), Tasks (actions), Commitments (rules you keep or break), Expectations (waits) and Infos (notes).

Why
Each kind answers a different question: do it, reach it, keep it, wait for it. Each resolves its own way.
Without
One "item" type with a flag for every behaviour; a Goal could be ticked off like a chore.
Lives
one table per kind; domains/ · tasks/ · infos/ · ADR 0005

The parent tree

Every node has exactly one parent. The tree is the board: views draw it, and context flows down it.

Why
Windows, the Agentic flag, privacy and archival all reach children through it, so a branch is set once.
Without
Every node carries all its own context, and moving a branch means editing every row in it.
Lives
parent_type / parent_id on each kind; tasks/ancestry.rs

Flows and Habits

A Flow is a template of Task, Goal, Commitment and wait items placed relatively in time. Starting it copies real nodes. A Habit is a Flow with a Recurrence: its instances are derived, never copied.

Why
Repeated structure without retyping it, and recurring work without a growing pile of rows.
Without
Weekly chores retyped every week, or thousands of stored copies a daily habit would leave behind.
Lives
flows/ (template.rs, habits.rs, rules/items.rs) · ADR 0002

Overlays

The difference between one Habit occurrence and its template: a status, a done time, any field edited on that one occurrence. An empty column means "as the template says".

Why
Storage grows with what you change, not with how many weeks pass, and editing the template still reaches every untouched occurrence.
Without
Touching an occurrence would freeze a full copy, cut off from later template edits.
Lives
task_/goal_/commitment_/expectation_overlays; nodes/overlay.rs
2 · What's derived

Rows that exist because something else does

Worked out on every load from the stored rows, and treated exactly like stored rows by everything downstream.

Derived nodes

Habit occurrences, a wait's check tasks, the wait an Asynchronous Task spawns when done, and the wait a delegated Task has on its person. Each is an ordinary row of its kind with an origin saying why it exists.

Why
Every view, filter and the MCP read one row shape; none needs a second code path.
Without
Each surface special-cases each source, and they drift apart.
Lives
nodes/ (origin, table, waits) · flows/occurrences.rs · ADR 0008

Three kinds of id

A stored row has an integer row id. Every node has a full id, a UUID. A short id is the shortest unique prefix of it, at least three characters, that is never a row id.

Why
Derived rows have no row number, and people need a handle shorter than a UUID that can't be mistaken for a row or PR number.
Without
Two id types in every request, and "224" meaning three different things.
Lives
nodes/id.rs · mcp/ids.rs · src/utils/uuid-v5.ts

Two status models

An ordinary Task is To Do, In Progress, Started or Done. A Task that reads as Agentic is To Do, On Agent, Review, Doing or Done. The two are separate types.

Why
The board has to say who holds the work. On Agent is the agent's; Doing is yours.
Without
An agent's work reads as yours, and fills Do and Zen with things you are not doing.
Lives
tasks/model.rs · tasks/rules/gestures.rs · src/utils/status-mapping.ts

Review

Never set: an On Agent Task with an open agent question beneath it. Answering the question puts it back On Agent.

Why
It is the only way an agent hands work back, and the question says what you need to do.
Without
Agent questions scatter as loose waits, and nothing on the Task says it is waiting on you.
Lives
tasks/rules/review.rs · mindmap/rules/facts.rs · src/utils/open-question.ts

Compound

A Task whose status is read off its sub-items: Done when they all are, In Progress or Started as they progress. Never stored, and you cannot set it by hand.

Why
A Task that is just the sum of its steps should never disagree with them.
Without
A parent says Done while a step is open, or To Do after everything finished.
Lives
tasks/compound.rs · flows/compound_readings.rs
3 · Time

Windows, slots, lateness and clocks

Time is arithmetic over a calendar, not rows in a table. Every time-based state is read off "now" at load.

Scopes and the 02:00 day

Season, Month, Week (Sunday to Saturday), Day, Part of Day and Exact windows, named by small value keys and never stored. Every canonical scope starts and ends at 02:00.

Why
A Day must contain its own Night (22:00 to 02:00); a scope contains exactly its parts.
Without
Between midnight and 02:00 the Day says "today" while its Night says "yesterday", and everything derived from it flips early.
Lives
scopes/ (key, derive, resolve) · ADR 0009

Time Scope and Plan

A Time Scope is when something matters; a Plan is the slot you mean to do it in, inside that window. An empty Time Scope inherits the nearest window above, and a Task with no Plan inherits the nearest Plan above, clipped to its own window.

Why
"Relevant this month" and "doing it Tuesday morning" are different facts that change at different rates.
Without
Either everything is scheduled rigidly or nothing knows when it stops mattering.
Lives
tasks/scope_rules.rs · tasks/rules/plan_inheritance.rs · ADR 0001

Timing

Pending before the window, Active in it, Lapsed after. Worked out from the effective window, own or inherited.

Why
Start should only offer what is in scope now.
Without
Next month's work and last month's both crowd today.
Lives
tasks/rules/lifecycle.rs

Due and Overdue

A due is the window whose end makes work late: one set on the Task, or by default its window under Keep Overdue. Overdue is a flag: unfinished, not archived, past the due's end. It shows as an amber border.

Why
Being late is separate from being relevant; late work stays live.
Without
Lateness had to be a resolution, which settled and hid work that was still open.
Lives
tasks/rules/lifecycle.rs (effective_due, derive_overdue)

Habit clocks

A Window clock lays iterations side by side; a missed one is Archived, carried into the next (Overdue), or left Owed. An Interval clock keeps one open instance and counts the Gap from completion.

Why
Habits fail differently: a missed run is gone, a missed bill is still owed.
Without
One rule that is wrong for half the habits.
Lives
flows/rules/habits.rs · flow_recurrences.clock, miss_policy

Cooldown and done times

A cooldown blocks the next iteration for N units after one is done; under Owed it blocks every open one. Every finish records when it happened, and a Task's done date can be corrected.

Why
Done on Saturday should not count again on Sunday, and a habit ticked late should count from when it was really done.
Without
Back-to-back completions across a window edge, and clocks counting from the moment you remembered to tick.
Lives
flows/rules/cooldown.rs · tasks/done_date.rs
4 · Lifecycle

Status says where the work stands; archival says whether it is in play

Kept apart on purpose, so neither has to grow values that belong to the other.

Resolution

How a passed window settled an item: Completed, or Missed under Archive. A Habit iteration resolves when every occurrence its template made is settled: done, compound ones by their derived status, a Commitment item's by a verdict, a wait item's by its release.

Why
A passed window needs an answer, and the Habit clocks count from it.
Without
Old work hangs on with no verdict, and clocks have nothing to count from.
Lives
tasks/rules/lifecycle.rs · flows/occurrences.rs (resolutions)

Archival and Backlog

Live, Backlog, Frozen or Archived. Set by hand (a Goal or Project's status; a Task's stored Archival, Live, Backlog or Archived; a Commitment's or wait's own archive), inherited (everything beneath a Task or Commitment archived by hand), or derived: a settled window archives, and so does a released wait whose window has passed. Delegation is not archival: it has its own pill.

Why
"Set aside" and "finished" are different questions; a backlogged Task keeps its real status. Putting a branch away is one write, and taking it back restores it exactly.
Without
Statuses multiply ("done-ish", "paused-forever"), every filter has to know them all, and archiving a project means archiving every step in it.
Lives
tasks/rules/lifecycle.rs · tasks/rules/archival.rs · filters/facts.rs

Commitment verdicts

A Commitment is judged, not done: Kept, Broken or still Unresolved. Its Verdict Window bounds how long the answer stays owed; past it, it Expires.

Why
A rule like "phone off by 23:00" is kept or broken, and an unanswered night may well have been kept.
Without
A missed check-in would count as a broken commitment.
Lives
tasks/commitments.rs · ADR 0005
5 · Inheritance

Set it once on a branch

Worked out on read by climbing ancestors, never copied down.

Agentic

A three-state flag (unset, yes, no) that inherits down and can be overridden. It decides the status model, and what the MCP may write. Each Agentic Task has its own brief, and needs a Spec before it can start.

Why
Mark a project for agents in one edit, and pull one Task back out of it.
Without
Flagging every Task by hand, and agents writing into your own work.
Lives
tasks/agentic.rs · src/utils/agentic.ts

Scope containment

A child's window sits inside its parent's, a Plan inside its window and inside the Plan it inherits, a due inside its window. Checked on every write; a write that leaves a Task with no Plan inside its window is refused, naming it; narrowing a parent offers to clamp its children (or, for Plans, clear theirs so they inherit).

Why
A step can't matter outside the window of the thing it is a step of.
Without
Children outliving their parents and plans scheduled after the work stopped mattering.
Lives
tasks/scope_rules.rs

Link inheritance

Time Scope, Plan and Agentic inherit today. Asynchronous deliberately does not. Tags, knowledge-base links and delegation are specified to inherit but not built.

Why
Context belongs on the branch; a filter on a parent's tag should find its children.
Without
Re-tagging every child, or filters that miss half a project.
Lives
docs/spec/link-inheritance.md

Privacy

A node's own Private flag hides it and everything beneath it, outside Private Mode and from the MCP even inside its roots.

Why
One switch keeps a whole area out of sight and out of agents' context.
Without
Hiding a subtree node by node, and leaking whatever was missed.
Lives
access/ · mindmap/rules/facts.rs
6 · Blocks

Why something can't start yet

A block always carries its reason. Blocks gate starting work; they never change a status.

Block reasons

An ordered list of reasons written on a Task or Goal by hand.

Why
Some obstacles are not on the board ("waiting for the landlord").
Without
Blocked work looks startable.
Lives
block_reasons/

Dependencies

A Task can depend on a Task, a Goal or a stored wait. Until it is met, the Task reads "Blocked by task 6f3 (title)". Cycles are refused.

Why
Order between pieces of work is a fact worth recording once.
Without
You remember the order yourself, and Start offers things out of turn.
Lives
tasks/mod.rs · mindmap/rules/facts.rs · src/utils/blocked-by.ts

Derived blocks

Blocks no one writes: the agent capacity lock on every unfinished Agentic Task, a Habit's cooldown, and a compound Task whose open sub-items are all blocked.

Why
One mechanism, so filters, badges and the MCP need nothing new for each.
Without
A special filter rule per feature, each one missing a view.
Lives
capacity/rules/blocks.rs · flows/rules/cooldown.rs · tasks/compound.rs
7 · Reading it out

One board, many readers

You and an agent must never disagree about what is live.

Presets

All, Plan, Start, Do, Backlog and Unblock: each a rule over derived facts. Start, for example, drops blocked, pending, lapsed (but not Overdue) and planned-ahead work.

Why
"What can I begin now?" should have one answer, everywhere.
Without
Each view invents its own idea of "now".
Lives
filters/ (Rust) · src/utils/filter-tree.ts, list-filter.ts

The conformance pair

Every rule the views answer per keystroke is written twice, in a Rust rules module and as a TypeScript copy, and one shared case file per rule family runs against both.

Why
The views filter instantly on every keystroke, without a round trip; the corpus keeps the two copies honest.
Without
The agent's board and yours drift apart silently.
Lives
conformance/ · *-conformance.test.ts · ADR 0010

Pills and the focus exemption

Filters combine as Any, All and Not. The selected node stays on screen, dimmed, even after your own edit stops it matching.

Why
Completing a Task under Plan should not make it vanish from under the cursor.
Without
Every edit risks losing your place.
Lives
src/utils/filter-modes.ts, focus-exemption.ts

Views

Mindmap (the tree), List (rows), Plan (triage into scopes), Steps (one level at a time) and Zen (what's in progress). One tree and one filter model under all of them.

Why
Different jobs need different shapes of the same truth.
Without
Five boards to keep in step.
Lives
src/components/*View · docs/spec/*-view.md

The MCP

Agents' door to the board. They see only inside the roots you open, never what is private, and write only Agentic Tasks. They hand work back by asking a question.

Why
A clean, bounded context for an agent, on the same derived board you see.
Without
Agents read everything, or a separate copy that disagrees.
Lives
mcp/ · access/ · capacity/

The server

arlesh-server serves the board over HTTP, as the first step toward multi-device. It runs the core through the Python bindings and adds no rules of its own. Every request carries a bearer token, which names the client the journal records. It serves the MCP at /mcp too, and holds the database while it runs, as the app does: it will not start while the app or another server holds it, unless forced.

Why
One copy of the board, reachable from more than one device, with one writer.
Without
The board lives only inside the running app on one machine.
Lives
crates/arlesh-py/python/arlesh/server · arlesh[server]
8 · Keeping it honest

Trustworthy history and a truthful board

The board is only useful if what it says is true and every change can be taken back.

The undo journal

SQL triggers on every table record each row's before and after. One gesture is one step; Ctrl+Z replays it in reverse, across windows. An agent's or another client's writes are journaled but never undone from the app.

Why
A trigger can't be forgotten by a command that doesn't know it exists.
Without
An inverse to write for every one of eighty-odd commands, and the next one missing it.
Lives
undo/ · scripts/generate-undo-triggers.sh · ADR 0006

Clients and a second writer

Every write is journaled with the client it came through: the app is desktop, and a Python session names itself. The app's Ctrl+Z takes only its own. While the app runs it holds the database, and another writer must force its way in; reading is never refused.

Why
More than one program writes the board now, and each must tell its own changes from another's.
Without
Ctrl+Z in the app would reverse what a script did, and a script could rewrite rows under the app's open windows unwarned.
Lives
database/ (client, hold, open) · migration 0092 · crates/arlesh-py

Migrations

Numbered schema steps, applied in order and never edited once applied. Data is moved aside, never dropped.

Why
Your real database must reach every new shape without losing anything.
Without
Upgrades that refuse to start, or quietly discard history.
Lives
crates/arlesh-core/migrations/

A truthful board

Nothing derived is stored, so nothing derived goes stale. Every window hears about every change. An agent whose next step is yours must say so with a question.

Why
You act on what the board says; a board that lies costs more than none.
Without
Stale copies, windows that disagree, and work that looks held when it is waiting on you.
Lives
board.rs (board-changed) · AGENTS.md
