# Habits

*One area of the [Arlesh design specification](../../SPEC.md).*

A **Habit** is a Flow with a **Recurrence** pattern (a flow becomes a Habit when given a Recurrence — stored in a `flow_recurrences` row keyed by the flow, whose presence marks the flow as a Habit; a Habit requires a scoped flow). Its instances are generated automatically and are **derived rows** of their kinds' tables ([Derived nodes](virtual-nodes.md)): each is identified by `(instance, iteration scope, cycle pair)` — where an instance is a flow item **or the flow root itself** (the root materializes as a normal Task/Goal, so it is a first-class completable instance, not merely an aggregate of its items; an item-less habit therefore still has one instance, its root) — and drawn from the template, with only divergences (status, edited fields, relations, archival) persisted, in its kind's **overlay**. An occurrence is otherwise an ordinary Task, Goal or Commitment, on every surface and to every gesture; what differs for it is listed in [Derived nodes](virtual-nodes.md). The **cycle pair** is part of that key because a flow item with N pairs draws N instances in a single iteration, exactly as it materializes N items when the flow is started: a morning and an evening occurrence of the same item are two things to complete on the same day, and ticking one must not tick the other. The root, and any item with no pairs, key on the `0` sentinel. The root instance's title reads `{flow title} {start scope}` (e.g. "Exercise W22").

**Recurrence** = **Repetition** + a **Clock**:

- **Repetition** — a Start anchor, an optional **Gap** of N of a scope kind, and an optional end.
- **Clock** — what decides when the next occurrence falls (ruled by the user, 2026-09-30; Task #245, which replaced the Consumption tree — see [*Clocks*](#clocks) below):
  - **Window** — iterations tile from the Start anchor, one per Flow Window plus the Gap (a Gap kind no finer than the habit scope), whether or not the last was done. What becomes of one that passes unfinished is its **miss policy**:
    - **Archive** — it lapses: Missed, and archived with its window. It is never Overdue.
    - **Owed** — it stays open, due at its own window, so it is flagged **Overdue** past it, and the iterations after it keep coming beside it. Owed work stays **in view**: a passed iteration still open is never folded into the Habit's collapsed history, but drawn beside it (see *Collapsed history* below; ruled by the user, 2026-10-01).
    - **Overdue** — it is archived as Missed, and the iteration open now **carries** it: that iteration's relevance reaches back to the first missed window, it is due there, and its root reads "Water the plants W3 from W1" until one is completed.
  - **Interval** — **one open instance** at a time. The Gap counts from completion: the next instance's window starts the unit after the one the last was completed in, plus the Gap — a two-week window W1–2 completed in W3 gives W4–5, completed in W1 gives W2–3. The open instance stays Overdue past its window until it is done. An Interval Habit's flow may be **Unscoped**: its instances have no window and are never Overdue, and the next one appears the Gap after the day the last was completed (with no Gap, the moment it is).

<a id="creating-a-habit"></a>**Creating a Habit.** A Habit is made by switching **Repeating** on in a Flow's editor, or in one step with **`Shift+H`** on the Mindmap and in the Steps View (and from the Step's "+"): the blank Flow editor `Shift+F` opens, headed *New Habit*, with Repeating already on. It starts at the defaults the switch gives an existing Flow — the Flow's own defaults (a Task instance, a one-week window) and a Recurrence starting at the current Day's scope of the window's kind, with no Gap, no end and a **Window** clock that **Archives** what it misses — so the user sets the recurrence before anything is written. Saving writes the Flow and its Recurrence as **one all-or-nothing Gesture**: a refused Recurrence leaves no plain Flow behind, and one `Ctrl+Z` takes the Habit back whole. Switching Repeating off before saving creates a plain Flow instead. Switching the window to **Unscoped** keeps the Habit and sets its clock to **Interval**, the one clock an Unscoped Habit can keep: the editor drops the clock choice. A Habit is parented by a Flow's rules — under an Aspect, a Domain, a Project or a Goal — and `Shift+H` anywhere else is refused by name, as every typed create is.

<a id="the-recurrence-section"></a>**The editor's Recurrence section** (ruled by the user on review, 2026-10-01: "make the editor section sparser … and the clock pick before gap and ends"; the folding summary that round also built was then dropped — "redesign is good enough, we can leave it without expand"). Under the *Repeating (Habit)* switch the controls are always shown in full, in this order: the **clock** (Window / Interval), then the **miss policy** (*If missed*: Archive / Overdue / Owed) — present only under a Window, hidden rather than greyed under an Interval — then **Starts**, **Gap** and **Ends**. Each choice's one-line explanation is its **tooltip**, not text on the page, and the section has no heading of its own beyond the switch.

**Generation.** Iterations are **derived**, never persisted per iteration: a pure function of the Recurrence, the reference day, and the completed iterations (the only persisted facts — a completed instance carries a `resolved_at`). Each started iteration is classified `Active` / `Done` / `Lapsed` (Window + Archive, passed unfinished) / `Missed` (Window + Overdue, passed unfinished and carried by a later one). Only iterations whose window has begun are generated; future ones are not drawn (a node standing in for them is `Arlesh-9md`). A day-and-up window begins and ends at 02:00 (see [*The day boundary*](time-scopes.md)), so a daily Habit turns over then and not at midnight: at 01:30 yesterday's iteration is still the open one. An iteration is *resolved* when every one of its (non-tombstoned) instances is done. Clock semantics: Window + Archive lapses any past unfinished iteration and keeps only the current window active; Window + Owed keeps every started iteration active until done; Window + Overdue keeps the **latest started** iteration open — however long ago its own window passed, under a Gap or past the Habit's end — and classifies every earlier unfinished one Missed; an Interval keeps its one open instance active until done, and draws nothing after it. **Each instance carries its own window.** A flow item's Cycle Scope resolves against *that iteration's* window start — backend-side, through the same offset arithmetic `start` uses, so a rendered occurrence and a started one cannot disagree about when "the 2nd day of week 3" falls — and the instance's Timing is read from it rather than from the iteration's. A Morning item inside a day-long iteration is Active during the morning and, under Window + Archive, Lapsed from noon; a Tuesday item inside a week-long one is Active on Tuesday alone. Sub-day windows obey the **clock** like every other size: under every clock but Window + Archive a passed *unfinished* occurrence stays open, and comes due, rather than lapsing, and if a morning routine should vanish at noon, Archive is what says so. An occurrence whose window has not opened is **Pending**, and whether it renders is the **status preset's** decision, not generation's: **All shows it** — All's contract is that it shows everything, and a Habit's later-today items are exactly what one looks at All to see — while **every other preset hides it** (**Plan**, **Start**, **Do** and **Backlog** alike), together with its own subtree, so this evening's item does not sit among the morning's work (children nest under their parent's first occurrence and would otherwise be redrawn a level up). Backlog is no exception: an unopened window is not work that was set aside, and the preset that exists to show what you put down has nothing to say about what has not started. The subtree rule holds on every surface it is drawn on — the Mindmap prunes it, the List View's task rows walk their ancestors for it, and so does the **Commitments band** above them, so a Commitment hung on an unopened occurrence never outlives the occurrence it belongs to. The occurrence is generated and sent either way; dropping it backend-side put it beyond every preset at once. The rule is scoped to the occurrences a Habit generates in bulk: a hand-made task scheduled for next week is Pending too and still plans, as it always has. (Exact-time `HH:MM–HH:MM` cycles stay deferred with the cycle editor that would enter them.) A Window + Archive iteration's occurrences (root and items alike) map onto the Task/Goal Archival model in [*On-exit behavior*](time-scopes.md) once their window has passed — a past-window instance is *effectively archived* regardless of whether it's `Done` or `Lapsed`, so a habit occurrence with some items completed and others missed still archives as a unit, rather than partially lingering visible via its completed siblings. **Done work never piles up, whatever the clock.** Staying open is about *unfinished* work: once an occurrence's own window has passed, a done Task occurrence or an achieved Goal occurrence reads **Lapsed**, its Resolution **Completed**, and — per [*On-exit behavior*](time-scopes.md) — its effective Archival **Archived**, exactly as a stored Task or Goal does; only the unfinished ones stay Active and accumulate. An iteration's **root** closes with its iteration: once the iteration is `Done` and its window has passed, the root reads Completed and archives too, while a root ticked off above work still open stays live alongside that work. Until the window passes, a done occurrence stays where it is. A **commitment** Habit's supporting steps are the exception: while the verdict is owed they stay with the iteration, and the Verdict Window is what archives them (see *Commitment habits* below).

**Display.** Instances render under the Target Node — the Flow's own when it has one, otherwise its parent (and, only if that parent is not in the rendered tree, the Flow node itself). Active and past instances render directly; future instances are not drawn. (A node standing in for them, and pinning one out of it, is tracked as `Arlesh-9md`.)

**Collapsed history.** At the other end of the same timeline, a **collapsed node** stands in for a run of **passed** iterations — those whose window has closed, which the backend states per iteration as its exclusive `window_end` rather than leaving the renderer to redo the Repetition's offset arithmetic. *Every* passed iteration folds, however it ended: Done as much as Lapsed, Missed and Expired, because the pile-up is the problem and a kept daily habit accumulates fastest of all — three hundred and sixty-five nodes a year. The iteration whose window is still open always renders on its own, and so does **owed** work — under Window + Owed, a passed iteration that is still open (its origin's `iteration_scope.owed`, which the backend sets for a work Habit only; a commitment Habit's unanswered nights fold as before): it is taken out of the run it sits in and drawn right after the run node, so the iterations around it still fold together, and a run left shorter than the threshold by taking it out is drawn as its own iterations. Archive and Overdue are untouched by this, and Window + Archive needs no rule of its own here: its Lapsed iterations fold the moment the next window begins. The node reads its Habit's title and then its tally — "Journal: 14 passed · 9 done, 5 missed", where *done* means finished as intended, which for a commitment Habit is **Kept** — and names its day span in a tooltip. The title is on the node because a run stands among the Habit's own live iterations and, under a busy target, among other Habits' runs: a tally alone says what happened but not to what. The **threshold** is a setting ("Collapse habit history after N", default 3, minimum 2) on the settings modal's **General** page; it applies to every Habit and to **both views that fold** — the Mindmap and the [Steps View](steps-view.md), which folds with the same code — and is an app-wide preference, not a per-tab one. (It sat on the Mindmap page until the Steps View began folding on 2026-09-27; the stored value carried over.) A run shorter than it draws its iterations directly.

`Ctrl+/` — the binding that collapses any other node — **expands** a collapsed run, and the expansion **persists with the tab** (an ordinary collapsed node does not: forgetting a collapse merely shows something again, where forgetting an expansion would silently re-fold what the user opened). The expansion is a **scope-levelled tree**, not a flat list: walking up from the iteration's own scope kind through day → week → month → season, a level is inserted for a kind only where the run spans **more than one** of that unit — five days inside one week give five day nodes and no week level, three weeks inside one month give a week level and no month level. **Year is a display-only grouping above Season**, keyed on the year a Season is labelled with so a Winter straddling New Year stays one node: nothing else changes, and there is no Year scope row, no Year in the picker and no Year-scoped items. Each intermediate level node carries its own tally ("September · 18 done, 12 missed") — its Habit is already named by the run above it — and **comes up collapsed in its turn**: opening a run is a request for the shape of the history, not for every day of it, so a year opens as four seasons rather than as three hundred and sixty-five nodes. Every level therefore answers to the same inverted set the run does — a habit-group node is drawn shut unless the user has opened it — rather than to `collapsedNodeIds`, whose absent-means-expanded reading is the wrong way round for anything the fold draws. Each opens independently with the same gesture.

`Ctrl+Alt+/` is the way past that one level at a time when the days themselves are what you want: it expands the selected node **and everything beneath it**, so a run opens with all its levels and all its iterations at once. It is one walk of the *drawn* tree rather than the loaded one — the fold's nodes have no counterpart in the loaded tree — and it needs no second pass, because a run's levels and iterations are built the moment the fold builds the run and are merely drawn shut. Going down, it writes to whichever set each node answers to: it clears ordinary nodes from `collapsedNodeIds` and adds the fold's nodes to the opened set. Each kind therefore keeps the persistence rule it already had — the fold's openings are written down with the tab, the ordinary expansions are not — rather than acquiring a third.

The same chord **shuts** a subtree again, and the mirror runs through both sets in reverse: ordinary nodes are added to `collapsedNodeIds`, the fold's nodes are removed from the opened set. Which way a press goes is decided by the node it was pressed on, and by that node alone — **drawn shut means open it, drawn open means shut it** — read off the single reconciled set the layout uses, so neither polarity is re-derived and a subtree that is a mixture of open and shut (which it usually is) cannot leave the gesture undecided. The pressed node's own state is what changes, so a second press is always the other direction. A collapse descends past that node rather than merely shutting it, because the two sets are also what `Ctrl+/` reads: leaving a run's levels open would make the next single-node press reveal every iteration instead of the levels. The consequence for what is written down is that a recursive collapse takes every group id in the subtree back out of the opened set, so opening and shutting a run leaves nothing persisted behind it.

A collapsed node is **selectable but not filterable**: arrow keys land on it and leave it — onto it from its neighbours, off it to them, and in and out of its scope levels — and it is otherwise a display node with no row, no status, no scope and no tags of its own — so the fold runs **after** the filter rather than before it, over whichever iterations the filter kept. It therefore renders exactly when at least one iteration behind it would have. Because it exists only in the tree the canvas draws and has no counterpart in the loaded one, **directional movement reads its candidates off the drawn tree**: a node the fold invented has no relatives in the loaded tree, and one the filter dropped is not a relative in the drawn one. Nothing new is persisted about iterations either: they stay derived, and the only stored state this adds is the threshold and which group nodes — runs and scope levels alike — are open.

**Added children.** An occurrence can be given **real children of its own**, attached to that occurrence and to no other. The occurrence stays derived — nothing materialises on first touch (ADR 0002) — and the children are ordinary rows keyed to the occurrence by its node key (`derived_children`), so storage stays proportional to divergences rather than to iterations. They are created with the same gesture used everywhere else — or moved there, by cut and paste or a drag — in any kind **its kind can parent** (for a Task: Task, Goal, Commitment, Expectation, Info), and are fully editable nodes: scopeable, taggable, plannable, completable, and able to hold children of their own in the ordinary way (only the first level is attached; everything below it is an ordinary parent link). Because an occurrence has no integer id for a parent column to hold, an added child's own `parent_type`/`parent_id` name the occurrence's **host** — the Target Node the occurrence renders under — while the attachment is what makes the occurrence its parent: containment, inheritance and Archival all follow the occurrence, and a child whose attachment were ever lost would surface beside the occurrence rather than nowhere. **Containment** applies normally: the child's Time Scope must sit within the occurrence's window (an occurrence's own, resolved Cycle Scope when a pair drew it, else the iteration's), and its Plan within its Time Scope. The occurrence's window is settled when the child is attached rather than re-derived on every read, so that the ancestry climb that enforces containment never has to resolve a Flow Window. A **Commitment** child is the one kind written already carrying that window as its own, because a Commitment with no effective window has nothing it could ever be kept or broken over; the other three are written unscoped and inherit it. A Commitment is configured before it exists — `Shift+C` opens the editor first — so its verdict, Verdict Window, privacy and tags are written onto the attached row before the board redraws, and it appears once, configured, rather than twice. Leaving the editor's window empty keeps the occurrence's; naming one narrows it, and a window the occurrence cannot hold is refused in the editor with nothing left behind.

**Iteration resolution is unchanged**: an iteration resolves when every one of its *template-derived instances* is done, and added children are not instances. A **compound** occurrence (a flow Task item marked Compound, or an occurrence switched to it — see [*Compound*](resources.md#compound)) is the one instance an iteration does not wait on: its status is drawn from its subtree, whose template-derived part must be done for the iteration anyway, and whose hung-on part never withholds one. Nothing hung on an occurrence can withhold an Interval Habit's next instance. What guards completion instead is a **prompt, with nothing stored**: marking an occurrence `done` while it still holds unfinished children is refused pending confirmation, and the refusal **names them** — a confirmation that can only be accepted blind is not consent. Confirming marks the occurrence done and leaves the children in place; declining writes nothing. A Task is unfinished until `done`, a Goal until achieved or archived, a Commitment until a verdict is recorded (kept **or** broken — either is an answer given), and an **Info is never unfinished**: a note is not work. The guard watches `done` alone; a commitment Habit's iteration is kept or broken, and a verdict is not a claim that anything under it was finished. Rejected: a per-child counts-toward-completion flag — it puts a permanent setting on every child to express something that matters at exactly one moment, and default-on it let a jotted note stall a habit indefinitely.

**Archival as a unit.** Added children take the occurrence's effective Archival with it: the occurrence's window governs them, on-exit **Archive**, so when the window passes they archive together rather than lingering beside a closed occurrence. Nothing is deleted and All still reaches them. An added child makes its occurrence **divergent**, so editing the Habit's scope or repetition raises the existing *archive-and-new vs delete-and-regenerate* prompt; delete-and-regenerate removes added children along with the instances they hung off, which is what the prompt exists to warn about. Deleting one never touches the template.

**Commitment habits.** An iteration of a Habit whose Instance Type is `commitment` renders as a **Commitment**, not as a Task or a Goal: the commitment glyph, the Verdict badge, and the two verdict controls in List View's commitments section — never a status control to cycle. It is selectable on the Mindmap like any other node, where `Enter` cycles its verdict and `X` records Broken, exactly as on a real Commitment; the tick and the cross remain List-View-only controls. That iteration is a Commitment row, and its Verdict is written on it as on any Commitment; clearing it back to Unresolved leaves nothing recorded on it. The items beneath the iteration are the flow's **task** items, rendered as Tasks — the supporting steps a Commitment legally parents — and, per [*Commitments*](resources.md), none of them ever gates the verdict. A commitment flow whose template holds a **flow goal** has no valid materialisation at all, since a Commitment holds no Goals: `start` is refused by `goals.parent_type`, and a Habit derives **no iterations** rather than drawing a subtree the model forbids — the Mindmap's load-condition banner names every flow it withheld, so nothing goes missing quietly. An iteration's Archival is derived here rather than by the lifecycle — past **and** judged is settled and archives; past and unjudged stays live, because the answer is still owed, and is never **Missed**. What ends "still owed" is the **Verdict Window**, which the Habit carries on the flow row (see *Recurrence* above) and every one of its iterations resolves to: the backend derives such an iteration as **Expired**, and it archives still unresolved. Expiry is checked before the past-and-unjudged rule, because under the Window + Owed clock a commitment Habit is fixed to, an unanswered iteration classifies Active rather than Lapsed right up until it expires. A Habit with no Verdict Window set keeps its iterations answerable indefinitely, exactly as a Commitment with none anywhere above it does.

**Editing** a Habit's scope, repetition or clock prompts (**Archive & new** — archive the old habit and create a new one — vs *delete instances and regenerate*) **only when divergent instances exist**; otherwise it silently regenerates. **Archive & new** clones the template onto a new flow, which takes the edit, and **archives the original**: it stops recurring after the Day the edit is made on (by the 02:00 boundary), so every iteration that had begun keeps its history and nothing later is generated. The clone, the archive and the edit are **one Gesture** — a single `Ctrl+Z` takes back all three, and a refusal part-way leaves none of them behind. (Archiving a Habit on its own, outside this prompt, is `Arlesh-9md`.)

## Clocks

Ruled by the user, 2026-09-30 (Task #245, part 3 of #241); it replaced the **Consumption** tree — Destructive vs Accumulating, Overlapping vs Blocking, and a Blocking Habit's catch-up policy (*all pending*, *next*, *latest*) — which no longer exists anywhere in the model. The clock is stored on `flow_recurrences` as `clock` (`window` / `interval`) and, for a Window, `miss_policy` (`archive` / `overdue` / `owed`), a `miss_policy` present exactly when the clock is `window`. A **commitment** Habit's clock is fixed to **Window + Owed** (it was Accumulating + Overlapping): Archive would conclude an unanswered night went unfinished, Overdue would fold it into the next, and an Interval has nothing to complete; its Verdict Window bounds it instead. The shape is kept open for the **Habit cooldown** (Task #242), which builds on the Window clock as a further miss policy or a Window option rather than a third clock.

**An occurrence's due.** An occurrence takes its default **due** from its Habit's clock, before any due of its own:

- **Window + Archive**: none — a missed occurrence lapses and archives instead.
- **Window + Owed**: its own window — the pair's Cycle Scope when a pair drew it, else the iteration's.
- **Window + Overdue**: its own window; for the iteration carrying a run of missed ones, the **first missed window**, for the root and every occurrence that shares the root's window (an item drawn by a pair keeps its pair's window).
- **Interval**: its window; none when the Habit is Unscoped.

A **backlogged** occurrence has no default due, exactly as a backlogged Task has none. An **explicit due** set on the occurrence wins over all of these (see *Explicit due* below). The Overdue flag then reads as it does for any Task ([*Due scope and the Overdue flag*](time-scopes.md#due-scope-and-the-overdue-flag)): unfinished, not effectively Archived, and past the end of its due — so an archived occurrence, Missed or set aside, is never Overdue. An Overdue occurrence may be planned past its window, as an Overdue Task may.

**The carried iteration ("W2 from W1").** Under Window + Overdue the iteration open now carries the unbroken run of Missed iterations just before it. Its root's **relevance** (its Time Scope) runs from the first missed window's start to the end of its own window, and its due is the first missed window. Which iterations are Missed is read off completions alone, so it is stable: a completed iteration keeps saying which windows it made up for, and nothing carries past a completion. The title is **drawn, not stored**: the backend sends the first missed window's start date on the root's origin, `origin.iteration_scope.missed_from`, and the Mindmap's iteration-root decoration draws `{title} {start scope} from {first scope}` through the `habits:carriesMissed` string, each scope formatted as the iteration label already is ("W3", "September", or the raw date for a sub-day window). The row's own title stays the Habit's, and that is what the editor edits — the label is how it is drawn, as "Exercise W22" always was.

**Interval.** The chain is derived, never stored: the first instance is anchored on the Start; each later one is placed by the completion of the one before it — the latest `resolved_at` among the iteration's instances, the instant its last instance was done, read from the overlays exactly as iteration resolution always read it. The **unit** a completion falls in is the window's own scope kind, read by the 02:00 day boundary (a completion at 01:00 on the 6th is still the 5th); a sub-day window's unit is the Day, so its band falls on the day after the completion. The next window never starts at or before the one it follows, so an instance completed before its window opened still moves the Habit on. An **Unscoped** Interval instance has no window: its value key is an Exact scope one second long at the instant it appears, which names it without being a window, its `window_end` is the sentinel `9999-12-31T23:59:59`, and a done one archives once its iteration is done, since the next has taken its place by then. **Un-completing** an Interval instance takes its successor away again: the chain stops at the first instance not done, so the next one is simply no longer derived. Anything written to that successor — an edit, a relation, an added child — stays stored, keyed by the window it had, and is ignored while no chain reaches that key; it comes back if a later completion places an instance on the same window again, and is otherwise reachable only in the database. Nothing is deleted.

**Explicit due.** An occurrence's own due is stored in its overlay (`task_overlays.due_scope_start_id` / `due_scope_end_id`, migration 0087) as a Task's is, and travels as the row's `due_scope`; `null` reads the default above. The Task editor's **Due** field is offered on every Task occurrence, naming *By its Habit's clock (default)* while there is no explicit due, and held to the occurrence's relevance — its own Time Scope, else its iteration's window (unbounded for an Unscoped Interval instance); a due outside it is refused (`due is not within the task's time scope`). A Goal occurrence has no explicit due, as a Goal has none.

**Migration (0087).** Every Destructive Habit became Window + Archive, every Accumulating + Overlapping one Window + Owed, and every Accumulating + Blocking one Window + Overdue, whatever its catch-up policy. Nothing else was rewritten: every overlay, relation and added child stays keyed as it was. What changes for a former Blocking Habit is only how its existing rows classify. Under Blocking the open iteration was the first unfinished one, and every later one was withheld; under Window + Overdue the iteration open now is the latest started one, and the earlier unfinished ones — the old open iteration included — are Missed and archived, with whatever they hold. On the user's board on 2026-10-01 that touched one Habit: flow 13's open iteration of the week of 2026-09-20, which held a Plan in its overlay, a released wait hung on it and a dependency on that wait, became Missed and archived with all three still stored, and the week of 2026-09-27 became the open iteration, "from" the 20th. Every other Blocking Habit classified the same before and after, except flow 12, which had no overlay at all. Row counts across the Habit tables were identical before and after on a copy of that database.

**The MCP.** `arlesh_flows.recurrence` returns `clock` and `miss_policy` in place of `consumption_kind`, `blocking_mode` and `catchup_policy`, and its description says what each clock and policy does. Occurrence rows in the snapshot carry `origin.iteration_scope.missed_from` when they carry missed windows, a `due_scope` when one was set explicitly, and `overdue` in their lifecycle as any Task does. `arlesh_tasks.update` may now set `due_scope` on a Habit occurrence, where it lands in the overlay and wins over its Habit's default.
